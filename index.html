New: innovation committees

Where ideas face a verdict.

Two committees for builders and founders. Pitch a startup to a panel, or work with people from other fields to create an idea together. Both run online on 24 and 25 November.

Pitch Desk

Pitch your startup to a panel

A panel of judges listens to each pitch and scores it. Your pitch should be about 5 minutes long and delivered as a formal presentation, with a prototype or website if you have one.

How it works
  1. You receive a fixed slot and join the conference at that time.
  2. You present your pitch to the panel in about 5 minutes.
  3. You can leave after your pitch and wait for the results.
The panel judges
  • Depth of idea
  • Practicality
  • Founder's caliber

Support and recognition: innovative ideas will receive incubation support, and participants will receive a certificate.

IEC

Build a startup idea with people from other fields

The Innovation and Entrepreneurship Council brings together delegates from different fields. They meet, connect and shape startup ideas together. The committee runs in the general MUN format, with its focus on innovation.

How it works
  1. Delegates from different fields meet and connect in committee.
  2. Together they develop a startup idea over the two days.
  3. At the end, each delegate pitches the idea they created to the chairs.
Delegates are judged on
  • The idea they created
  • Their pitch to the chairs

Support and recognition: innovative ideas will receive incubation support, and participants will receive a certificate.

What you take away

How these 2 days help you.

Everything here happens online on 24 and 25 November. Students from North and South India take part together, and whether you debate or chair, you leave with practice in skills that carry over to school, college and work.

Speak with confidence

You will deliver opening speeches, respond to points of information and defend your position in front of a full committee. Speaking to a room of people you have just met is hard at first, and two days of repeated practice makes it much easier.

Research a real agenda

Every committee works on a live issue, from global security and renewable energy to cyber conflict, Indian politics and startups. Preparing for it teaches you to find reliable sources, check facts and back every claim with evidence.

Negotiate and draft

Debate is only half the work. You will build alliances, bargain with delegates who disagree with you and turn the result into a written resolution or proposal that the committee votes on.

Meet new people

Your committee includes students from other schools and cities, from both North and South India. You will work in small groups with people whose regions, languages and views differ from yours, and leave with contacts you can stay in touch with long after the conference ends.

Try chairing

Executive Board members run a committee for two days. You manage the speakers list, keep time, rule on procedure and guide delegates fairly, which builds leadership and calm decision-making under pressure.

Pitch an idea

In the Pitch Desk and the Innovation and Entrepreneurship Council, delegates present business ideas, answer tough questions and get direct feedback from the room on what would work in practice.

Understand current affairs

Following a topic closely for two days gives you a clearer picture of how countries, institutions and markets make decisions, and helps you read the news with more confidence afterwards.

Think under pressure

Debate moves fast. You will learn to listen carefully, adjust your argument on the spot and stay respectful when a discussion gets heated, skills that help in exams, interviews and group projects.

Build a record

Taking part in a full conference adds real experience to your school or college profile, and gives you something concrete to talk about in applications and interviews.
